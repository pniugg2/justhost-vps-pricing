## 512 MB or 1 GB: which jobs fit

The RAM figure is only half the constraint at this price. The disk is the other half, and it bites sooner.

- **WireGuard or OpenVPN for one to three people.** 512 MB is enough. The kernel does the work; userspace memory barely moves.
- **Static site behind Nginx, or a low-traffic WordPress with caching.** 512 MB runs it. 1 GB gives you room for PHP-FPM workers and a small database without swapping.
- **Uptime monitor, cron scripts, a Telegram or Discord bot.** 512 MB is generous here.
- **Docker with two or three small containers.** 1 GB, and take the 20 GB disk over the 10 GB one. Container images and logs are what fill 5 GB, not your application.
- **Postgres or MySQL under real load, Elasticsearch, a CI runner, a game server, a mail server with spam filtering.** None of these belong on 512 MB or 1 GB. They fail on RAM first and disk I/O second.

Two failure modes show up repeatedly on ultra-cheap plans: oversubscribed hosts, where several tenants share the underlying hardware and throughput varies with your neighbours, and features that look standard but are billed separately or capped, including snapshots and automated backups. Budget for managing the server yourself, because support at this tier is usually limited to basic troubleshooting.

If the workload you have in mind needs sustained CPU rather than occasional bursts, the fix isn't a bigger plan at $3. It's accepting that this bracket doesn't sell guaranteed CPU. Step up to the 2 GB tier for headroom, or move to a different class of product entirely.

## The port speed is the spec nobody reads

JustHost advertises unmetered traffic on every plan, and the number that actually limits you is the port. VPS Promo runs 10 Mbps fair-share, quoted as roughly 4+ TB a month. 10 Mbps works out to about 1.2 MB/s on a single transfer, which is fine for a VPN tunnel carrying browsing traffic and painful for anything resembling a file transfer or a video stream.

Move up the list and the ceiling lifts: 100 Mbps on New Star, 200 Mbps on Double Star, 300 Mbps on Sirius and Arcturus, 400 on Alpha Centauri, 500 on Vega, 750 on Capella and above. The "fair-share" wording matters as much as the number. It means the port is shared, so peak-hour throughput can land below the headline figure when the node is busy.

For a personal VPN, a bot or a monitor, 10 Mbps is not the bottleneck. For a site serving images, a backup target, or anything that streams, the port is the first thing you'd upgrade.

## KVM, and why it matters at this price

JustHost labels its VPS line as KVM, and the configurator states it explicitly. That matters more than the RAM number for what you can install.

KVM gives the virtual machine fixed memory. 512 MB means 512 MB, and you can change kernel parameters, load modules, run Docker and set up WireGuard without arguing with the host. OpenVZ and LXC containers on the same price tier share a memory pool at the hypervisor level, which means your 1 GB can behave like less under pressure, and Docker or VPN setups are often restricted or blocked outright.

When you compare $2 offers elsewhere, virtualization type is the field to check before the price. A 2 GB OpenVZ container is frequently worse than a 1 GB KVM machine for anything that needs a real kernel.

## IPv4, IPv6 and shared addresses

JustHost prices the address type separately, which is a useful signal. IPv6-only configurations start at $1.60/mo; IPv4 + IPv6 starts at $2.60/mo. The 65-cent gap buys reachability. Large parts of the internet still connect over IPv4 only, including plenty of corporate networks and consumer ISPs, so an IPv6-only box works for experiments and private tunnels but is a poor choice for a public website or mail.

Shared versus dedicated IPv4 is the other address question in this bracket. A shared address puts you on the same IP as other customers, which affects mail reputation and any service that gates on IP. One $2-class plan from VPS-Mart, at $2.52/mo for 2 cores and 4 GB, lists a single shared public IPv4 and only moves to a dedicated address at its $7.99 tier. JustHost's plan lists don't spell out dedicated addressing on every line, so confirm it in the cart if mail or IP-sensitive logins matter to you.

## When a $2 VPS is the wrong buy

Skip this tier if any of the following is true:

- You're hosting a shop, a client site, or anything with an uptime commitment attached.
- You need a mail server that delivers reliably.
- Your workload pins a core for more than short bursts.
- You want someone else to handle patching and backups.
- You need Windows. JustHost's VPS pages list Linux configurations.

The $2 bracket sells a fully isolated machine with root access for the price of a sandwich. What it doesn't sell is spare capacity, priority CPU, or anyone to call when the node misbehaves.

**Can I host a website on the $1.99 plan?**
A static site, yes. A small WordPress install with caching, yes with care. The 5 GB NVMe disk is the binding constraint once you add images and logs, which is why Sirius at 20 GB is the safer starting point for anything database-backed.

**Will the price go up when I renew?**
The listed discount is conditional on paying for 12 months, so a shorter term costs the list rate. Check the renewal line in the cart for your chosen term before paying, because a promo-line price like the Special plans can change without notice.

**Does the $2 tier run Docker or a VPN?**
Yes on KVM, which is what JustHost lists for its VPS line. WireGuard fits in 512 MB. Docker fits in 1 GB with a 20 GB disk, not comfortably in 5 GB.

**Which datacenter should I pick?**
The one closest to your users or to you, since latency follows geography. If you're unsure, test the node for a month on monthly billing at the list rate rather than prepaying a year.

JustHost's terms aren't stated on the plan pages reviewed here, so read the order page before paying. On a $24 annual plan the exposure is small; on a $176 one it isn't.
